Tracing shows what happened while your AI application handled a request. Phoenix receives spans over OTLP, groups them into traces, and shows them as trace trees you can read in Observe.

Pick how you want to add tracing.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Auto-instrumentation" icon="bolt" href="/docs/phoenix/tracing/how-to-tracing/setup-tracing/setup-using-phoenix-otel">
    Install the OpenInference instrumentor for your model client or framework, attach it once, and calls are traced.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Manual instrumentation" icon="code" href="/docs/phoenix/tracing/how-to-tracing/setup-tracing/instrument">
    Wrap your own functions, tools, and agent steps in spans when auto-instrumentation does not cover them.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

To let a coding agent do it for you, run [`px setup`](/docs/phoenix/integrations/developer-tools/cli#connect-a-project-with-px-setup).

## How it fits together

Phoenix builds on two open standards. [OpenTelemetry](/docs/phoenix/tracing/concepts-tracing/otel-openinference/overview) creates and exports the telemetry, and [OpenInference](/docs/phoenix/tracing/concepts-tracing/otel-openinference/semantic-conventions) defines the AI attributes that make a trace readable. Because both are open, the same instrumentation also works with any OTel-compatible backend, and it gives Phoenix [30+ tracing integrations](/docs/phoenix/integrations).

Every span carries a span kind that tells Phoenix what operation it represents, such as `LLM`, `TOOL`, `AGENT`, or `RETRIEVER`. See [Span kinds](/docs/phoenix/tracing/concepts-tracing/otel-openinference/span-kinds) for all twelve and the attributes each one expects, and [Customize your traces](/docs/phoenix/tracing/how-to-tracing/add-metadata/customize-spans) to add your own.
