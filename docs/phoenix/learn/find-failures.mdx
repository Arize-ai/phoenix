## Step 2: Find the requests in Phoenix

Shopper feedback, such as a thumbs-down rating, can help you choose which requests to inspect. Your review notes then record what you found in those traces. Wonder Toys doesn't collect shopper ratings in this exercise, so you'll start with the requests you just ran.

- Open **wonder-toys → Traces** and choose a time range that includes your requests.
- Enter this [filter expression](/docs/phoenix/tracing/how-to-tracing/filter-expressions) in the filter bar and press **Enter**:

```python
'dinosaur' in input
```

The filter searches the request text. Open each matching run and select **Wonder Toys** at the top of the trace tree to read its **Input** and **Output**.

### Use feedback from your own app

Phoenix stores ratings as **[annotations](/docs/phoenix/tracing/llm-traces/how-to-annotate-traces)**, which attach a label, score, or explanation to a span, trace, or session.

- If your app saves ratings on spans, open your project's **Spans** tab.
- For an annotation named `user_feedback` with the label `negative`, enter:

  ```python
  annotations['user_feedback'].label == 'negative'
  ```

- Use the name and label your app sends. Open a matching span and read the rating and any explanation under **Annotations**, then compare the answer with the request as described below.
- Include some requests without negative feedback too, since shoppers may not report every problem.

To collect ratings from your own app, follow [Capture feedback](/docs/phoenix/tracing/how-to-tracing/feedback-and-annotations/capture-feedback).

## Step 3: Compare each answer with the request

For each answer, check:

- **Budget:** Are the recommended prices below the shopper's limit?
- **Suitability:** Do the products and listed age ranges match the request?
- **No match:** If no suitable product was found, does the answer say so clearly?

In one recorded run, the shopper asked for toys under \$25, but the assistant recommended a \$27.99 kit. The trace status was **OK** because the request completed successfully. That status doesn't tell you whether the recommendation was good.

With the **Wonder Toys** span selected, choose **[Take notes](/docs/phoenix/tracing/how-to-tracing/feedback-and-annotations/annotating-in-the-ui#adding-annotations)**, write what you observed, and select **Add Note**. For the recorded budget issue:

> The shopper asked for toys under \$25. The answer recommended the \$27.99 Dinosaur Explorer Kit while acknowledging that it was over budget.

Keep examples that meet the requirements too. They help you check later whether a fix changes behavior that already worked.

## Step 4: Choose a problem to investigate

Read your notes together and look for observations that describe the same problem. In our recorded examples, the agent recommended a more expensive toy after acknowledging the budget. That gives us a specific behavior to investigate: recommending products above the shopper's limit.

Choose a problem based on its impact and how often it appears in the requests you reviewed. This small practice set doesn't tell you how common a problem is across all users. If your answers meet the requirements, keep them as passing cases rather than inventing a failure.

Next, [investigate and label a failed run](/docs/phoenix/learn/investigate-failure) to practice checking budget compliance. You can follow the same steps whether your requests passed or failed.
